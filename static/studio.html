A guide to the workspace

Surveys and interviews that follow the answers.

ChoiceGap helps UX and market researchers turn a research brief into a study, explore participants’ answers and review the evidence. You can run a survey, an AI-moderated interview, or a survey followed by an interview. You review the questions and approve the topics before anyone takes part.

It is designed to explore the say–do gap: situations where what people say matters may differ from a specific choice or experience. That difference is a reason to ask more, rather than assume someone is inconsistent. Price, timing, trust, habit or other practical circumstances may explain the choice. Agreement between answers is useful too.

How a study works

  1. Ask: start with your brief.

    Describe the decision, the people you want to understand and any hypotheses. Add a concept or image if useful. Muse drafts survey questions and an interview guide. Edit the wording, answer types, order and follow-up topics, then publish the approved study.

  2. Compare: give the answers context.

    Ask about priorities and a recent experience, then use an optional hypothetical choice or reviewed task recording. If you include comparison tasks, you approve the possible changes in advance. Muse can select one change to explore while the other displayed details stay fixed.

  3. Explore: follow up on what matters.

    Muse can ask a limited number of follow-up questions within your approved topics. These might clarify an answer, explore a constraint or investigate another explanation. Interviews support typing and optional voice with an AI character. Participants review transcripts and can correct the moderator’s understanding.

  4. Synthesize: review findings against the evidence.

    Generate an analysis of completed sessions. Review the exact answers supporting each finding, participant corrections, possible explanations, product or research recommendations and limitations. Export the report and evidence together.

What you gain as a researcher

  • A first study draft you can edit, rather than a blank questionnaire.
  • Relevant follow-ups during collection, while the participant is still there to explain.
  • A way to explore competing explanations through approved comparisons.
  • Findings you can trace back to answers and corrections.

This brings preparation, collection and synthesis into one workflow. You retain responsibility for study quality and interpretation.

An example: sustainable delivery

A shopper rates environmental impact highly but describes choosing standard delivery because they were at work. The moderator explores the timing constraint. An approved comparison offers a more precise arrival window for the lower-impact option, keeping the other details fixed.

The researcher can see whether that hypothetical choice changes, read the shopper’s explanation and decide what to test next. This does not prove what would happen with a real purchase.

Where Meta Muse fits

Muse Spark drafts the study, moderates follow-ups, selects approved comparisons and synthesizes answers. Muse Voice transcribes spoken responses. Optional tools can add cited background research, concept drafts with Muse Image and participant-reviewed descriptions of task recordings. Those inputs remain distinct from participant findings.

What to keep in mind

ChoiceGap is a local research prototype. Reported experiences are self-reports; hypothetical choices are not real purchases; recording descriptions are not verified click records. Behavioural explanations are tentative, and the analysis may find insufficient evidence for a conclusion. Researcher time savings and research quality have not been validated with human users.

Briefs, stimuli, submitted answers and optional recordings go to Meta for processing. Submitted text is stored in this computer’s unencrypted database. This build has no researcher sign-in or remote participant hosting. Use fictional information when trying it.

Survey questions

All questions can be skipped. Choice tasks are hypothetical unless you collect real behaviour through a separate method.

Interview guide

Open questions give the moderator a guide. Follow-ups respond to each participant's answers.

Test which conditions change a choice

Review a hypothetical baseline choice and a few possible changes. Muse selects one approved change after hearing the participant. The code keeps every other detail fixed. These short tasks explore choices; they do not prove a real-world causal effect.

Ground the interview in a task recording

Optionally invite a screen recording before the interview. Muse proposes visible events, and the participant can confirm or correct them. Raw video is not saved by this app.

How will people take part?

Muse Voice can transcribe while participants speak and detect the end of an answer. If streaming is unavailable, recorded answers remain available. Participants review text before submitting. Audio goes to Meta and is not saved by this app.

The 3D character runs locally, without another account. All moderators are identified as AI.
