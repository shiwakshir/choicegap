What participants will answer

They rate how much each delivery detail matters and whether they usually try to choose lower-impact delivery. They can explain their ratings.

They make four delivery choices, followed by a repeat if they selected an option. Then they explain one choice and say what might change it.

They can correct the AI's description of their reasoning. Two more choices each change one detail, followed by a final optional explanation. Questions can be skipped.

Adaptive study design and analysis
Review the participant follow-up protocol

After four adaptive choices and an optional reversed repeat, participants can explain one choice, describe circumstances that might change it, and confirm, correct or reject a tentative interpretation. Two variations each change one detail from that same task using these approved levels. A final optional question explores the choices across versions.

Questions: “What mattered when you made this choice? You can also describe anything the options missed.” “What circumstances, if any, could lead you to choose differently? It is fine if none would.” “Does this description reflect your reasoning? You can correct it or leave it unresolved.” “What, if anything, explains your choices across these versions? You can also describe something we missed.”

Review both levels for every factor below: any differing factor may be varied in one of the two follow-up comparisons. Muse can interpret the full session, propose which approved comparison to show first, then reconsider after the responses. With Muse enabled, survey responses, exact choices, interview answers, corrections and factor definitions are sent to Meta in at most four calls. Participant-selected eligible factors take precedence; the same two comparisons remain in the study. The participant can reject the initial description. Free text is stored and exported. These comparisons do not establish causality.
