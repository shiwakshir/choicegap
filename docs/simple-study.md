# A simple sustainability research study

## Research question

Why do shoppers who say sustainability matters still choose standard grocery delivery?

Imagine an online supermarket deciding whether to change its greener-delivery price, delivery window or supporting evidence. This is an example brief, not a claim about collected customer data.

## Method

1. **Survey:** ask how important sustainability, price, timing and trust are, plus whether the participant tries to choose lower-impact delivery.
2. **Choice exercise:** show realistic hypothetical delivery options. For example, standard delivery is free while a lower-impact option costs £3, with the other details held fixed in that pair. Other tasks compare timing and supporting evidence.
3. **Short written interview:** ask what shaped the choice, what might change it and whether the AI understood the explanation correctly. Compare two follow-up versions and ask the participant to explain their responses.
4. **Findings:** show the stated priorities, exact choices, participant explanations, corrections and the changes that coincided with a different selection.

A participant might say environmental impact matters, select free standard delivery, explain that the fee exceeds their budget, and select a lower-impact option when the fee is removed. That makes affordability a useful explanation to investigate; it does not mean sustainability is unimportant or establish a cause from one person.

The app currently conducts written AI interviews. It does not conduct live human-led in-depth interviews or observe purchases. Its findings compare what participants say with choices they make inside the study.

## Use the simpler page

Restart the server after updating, then open http://127.0.0.1:8000/?mode=researcher.

1. Select **Set up this study**.
2. Review the questions, delivery details and response-use notice. Confirm and select **Create participant link**.
3. Open the participant link in another tab on the same computer and complete the study.
4. Return and select **View findings**. Download the findings if needed.

The prepared draft contains no invented participant evidence and makes no model call during setup. All previous approval and participant safeguards remain. Muse continues its existing bounded interpretation/planning/review loop during an enabled interview.

The researcher's page explains the research question. Participants receive the neutral title **Grocery delivery preferences**; the title does not suggest that they ought to choose greener delivery.

Evidence import, editable factors, design review and older results are retained under **More settings and detailed research tools**. A draft with imported evidence or unsaved detailed edits must be reviewed through that detailed approval flow; the simple preview cannot silently approve it.

The localhost participant link works on the computer running the app. Inviting remote participants requires making the application available online; this change does not deploy it.
